I'm a software engineer who loves building things that matter - both in code and with circuits. My journey has taken me from robotics competitions to Nordic startups, and I've loved every moment of learning, building, and breaking things to understand how they work.

## 🔧 The Tinkerer in Me

If there's a device that can be opened, I've probably tried to open it. If there's a protocol that can be sniffed, I've probably tried to understand it. My curiosity drives me to **reverse engineer systems** - not to break them, but to understand the brilliant engineering behind them.

### Electronics & Hardware Projects

- **Microcontroller enthusiast**: Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi - building IoT devices, home automation, and experimental projects
- **Circuit design**: From breadboard prototypes to understanding datasheets and component selection
- **Sensor integration**: Working with various sensors (temperature, motion, environmental) to collect and process data
- **Protocol hacking**: Understanding serial communication, I2C, SPI, and how devices talk to each other

### Robotics Background

Participated in the **World Robotics Olympiad** - an experience that taught me problem-solving under pressure, mechanical design principles, and the importance of robust code when hardware is involved.

## 🔒 Deep Dive: Cryptography & Security

I'm fascinated by how cryptography keeps our digital world secure. Through my **DENS (Data Encryption & Network Security)** blog series, I explore:

- Classical ciphers and cryptanalysis (Vigenère, substitution, etc.)
- Modern encryption algorithms and their implementations
- Network security protocols
- Practical cryptography in real-world applications

It's not just theory - I implement these algorithms, break them, and understand why they work (or why they don't).

## 🌱 How I Learn

I'm a firm believer in **learning by doing**:

1. **Read the documentation** (or reverse engineer if there isn't any)
2. **Build something small** to test understanding
3. **Break it** to see what happens
4. **Fix it** and understand why it broke
5. **Share what I learned** through blog posts or projects
